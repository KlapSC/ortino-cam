# Shape and pattern generator in the browser (since 0.15.0)

The **shape and pattern generator** is a free tool in the community portal: [ortino-cam.com/tools/generator](https://ortino-cam.com/tools/generator). You need **no account and no installation**. You set up a pattern or a shape for a CNC router or laser, see the preview at once and whether it can be made with your bit, and download it as **SVG** or **DXF**. You can mill the file in ortino.CAM or in any other software that reads SVG or DXF. The generator produces **no G-code**.

New since 0.16.0: the **word and logo collage** (words and logos, three typefaces, see below). Templates come as a file from the program and go back to it as a file.

## What it can do

* **Tab “Patterns”:** 38 of the 40 pattern kinds of the Pattern Studio, 78 of the 80 templates with preview image (honeycombs, stars, Voronoi, chevron, fish scales, kagome, spirals, word and logo collage and many more; with search and categories). You set width and height (10 to 1200 mm per side, together at most 360,000 mm², for example 600 × 600 or 1200 × 300 mm), the random value (same value, same pattern) and the settings of the kind. **Text from holes** and **Image as hole grid** are missing: they need a typeface or an image that the service does not accept for these kinds; for those there is the [Pattern Studio](Drawing-and-Importing) in the program. The word and logo collage is the exception (next item).
* **Word and logo collage (since 0.16.0):** The kind **“Word and logo collage”** fills an area densely with up to four words or logos, in many sizes and rotations, interlocked. Small parts also sit in the inner areas of large letters (“o”, “A”, “0”). For each of the **four slots** you choose Off, Word or Logo. The same inputs and the same random value give the same picture. For this kind the area may be up to **1200 × 600 mm** (720,000 mm²); the smallest part height cannot be set below **4 mm**. There are four templates: *Name collage*, *Two words, dense*, *Word and logo* and *Four brands mixed*.
  * **Words:** Text of up to 40 characters (letters, digits and punctuation), plus typeface, bold and the **frequency** (1 to 5): It determines how often the word appears compared with the others.
  * **Typefaces:** **Montserrat** (slim, the default for new words), **DejaVu Sans** (strong, wide) and **DejaVu Serif** (with serifs). There are no more here because the server does not read system fonts (in the program there is also every system font). Montserrat has only the characters of Western Europe (accents, umlauts, digits, punctuation, euro); for Cyrillic or Greek you use DejaVu Sans. If a typeface lacks a character, the page says so with a solution and does not insert a placeholder box.
  * **Logos and images:** PNG or JPEG files, at most 400 KB each, up to four. The page checks the file in the browser before it sends anything. The dark part is the shape; with transparency the opaque part is the shape; **“Swap light and dark”** reverses that. At the logo slot there is the notice: *Only use logos and images you have the rights to.* Anyone who enters other people's names and logos is responsible for the rights.
  * **Millability:** Letters with inner areas (“o”, “A”, “0”) produce **islands** that fall out when cutting through. That is why the traffic-light check for words is almost never green; it names the islands and offers the fix **“Add bridges”**. The slim typeface has thin strokes: For a bit of 3.175 mm many openings are too narrow. The collage is meant rather for fine tools (V-bit, engraving) or a smaller bit, which you set in the traffic-light check. The templates use 1 to 2 mm web and parts from 8 mm height.
* **Millability traffic-light check:** You enter the **diameter of your bit** and the **minimum web**. The page reports “Good to mill”, “Millable with notes” or “Not millable like this”, gives measurements (thinnest web, narrowest opening, islands, cut length) and offers one-click fixes (omit openings that are too small, widen or merge thin webs, round inner corners to the bit radius, bridges). The milling time is an **estimate**. The page's preset (bit Ø 3.175 mm, minimum web 2 mm) is an assumption of the page, not of the pattern: Set your own bit.
* **Tab “Shapes”:** ten basic shapes (rectangle, rounded rectangle, circle, ellipse, polygon, star, heart, gear, rosette, slot) with dimensions in millimetres. The shapes are created **in the browser** and also work without the server. Notes on the bit show what the round bit cannot mill out of a shape (sharp inner corners, tooth gaps that are too narrow, bores that are too small). The gear is a decorative shape, not a power-transmission gear.
* **Download:** **SVG** (scale 1:1 in millimetres, layers for openings and outline), **DXF** (unit millimetre; the default is polylines with arcs, for older programs there is “Single lines and arcs”) and **PNG** (for viewing). **Arcs stay arcs**, not many small segments. The inner openings come first, the outline last; you can leave the outline out.
* **Custom templates, saving and importing:** The category **“My own”** stores your settings with **“Save as my own template”** in the browser. For **exchange** with the program there is the format `ortino-vorlage`: a file `*.ortino-vorlage.json`, the same in the program and in the browser. You can **save a template as a file**, with **“Save all”** all of them at once, and **import** files (file selection, at most 1 MB). Files from the program (from 0.15.0) go in here, and the program reads the files from here under “Own → Import” (see [Custom templates](Custom-Templates)). On import the page takes over patterns and shapes; it skips relief templates (there are no reliefs here), rejects patterns made from an image, and limits values outside the ranges with a note “Not applied: …”. Of the collage, a template stores **only the words, never an image**: A pattern with a logo cannot be saved as a template (the page gives the reason), but it can be downloaded as SVG or DXF. The program reads a file with a collage only from version 0.16.0. The templates exist only in this browser, so save them as a file.

## What it cannot do

* **No G-code.** G-code is only created in ortino.CAM: import SVG or DXF (see [Drawing and importing](Drawing-and-Importing)), choose the bit and the operation (for example pocket or profile cut), check, simulate, and only then mill.
* Apart from the collage, no text and image patterns, no rotation, no tab for reliefs. A button “Open in ortino.CAM” does not yet exist for patterns; you save the file and import it, or you import the template under “Own”.
* No statement about whether a pattern holds in your material. That depends on wood, bit, machine and clamping. **Try it on an offcut.**

## Limits of the service

The portal's server calculates the patterns. To keep it available for everyone, limits apply (portal defaults): at most **60 calculations per minute** and address, at most four calculations at the same time in the whole server, and after **10 seconds** a calculation is aborted. The page then shows a message with a solution (smaller area, larger cells, fewer fixes, or calculate the pattern in the program) and the last preview stays on screen, dimmed. For the collage there are additionally: at most four words or logos, 40 characters per word, 400 KB per image and about 1.25 MB per calculation for everything together.

## Privacy

* The page needs **no account**, sets **no cookies of its own** (the portal's cookies apply, see the privacy policy) and loads **nothing from third parties**.
* **Shapes** and the files to download are calculated or created in the browser; nothing goes to the server.
* **Patterns** are calculated by the server from the values you set (pattern kind, size, random value, settings as numbers, material thickness for the time estimate). For the **word and logo collage**, the entered **texts** and the **logo images** are part of this; the browser reads the logo only into the page's working memory and stores it nowhere. The server uses everything only for this one calculation and writes it **neither to a database nor to a file nor to the operating log**. The operating log contains for each request only the method, path, status code, duration and a request number, **not the IP address and not the content**. In working memory the server keeps only the counters of the rate limiting per address (gone after a restart) and a few recently calculated patterns (only the finished outlines, without texts and images). An upstream web server can log accesses with the IP address, as on any page of the portal; details are in the [portal's privacy policy](https://ortino-cam.com/privacy).
* **Custom templates** are written by the page into your browser's storage (Local Storage, two entries: `ortino.vorlagen.v1` for the list and `ortino.vorlagen.v1.vorschau` for the small preview images) **only after your click** on “Save as my own template” or when importing a file. The server learns nothing about it. They are deleted in the category “My own” or in your browser's site data.
* **Test entry:** When opened, the page uses a test entry (`ortino.vorlagen.v1.probe`), which it deletes again immediately, to check whether your browser's storage is usable. If it is not (private window, blocked), the templates last only as long as the page is open.
