ortino.CAM
CAD/CAM for CNC routers

From design to workpiece in one application: draw, calculate toolpaths, simulate in 3D, check and send straight to the machine. For Windows and GRBL controllers such as the Ultimate Bee with xPRO V5.

In three steps to the workpiece

The interface leads through five steps: Project, Design, Machining, Check, Cut. In easy mode only the most important settings are shown; everything else is under “More settings”.

1 · MACHINING

Define toolpaths

Profile, pocket, adaptive clearing, drilling, V-carving, inlay and 3D relief, with guide values for feed rate and spindle speed and a short explanation of why.

2 · CHECK

Simulate and check

Play back the material removal in 3D, find rapid collisions and oversize, and get a checklist with suggested solutions. If there are errors, export and machine start are blocked.

3 · CUT

Straight to the machine

Connect over USB or TCP, work zero and probing, tool-change assistant, pre-flight check before every run, pause, stop and start from a line.

More from the program

Screenshots of the interface; the machine in the “Cut” step is the built-in simulator; names, prices and values in the examples are made up.

Word and logo collage

In the Pattern Studio the new kind fills an area (for example a wall panel of 1200 × 600 mm) densely with up to four words, texts or logos, in many sizes and rotations and interlocked; small parts also sit in the inner areas of large letters (“o”, “A”, “0”). The form Words and logos has four entries (Off, Word or Logo): text up to 40 characters, typeface, bold, a logo from a PNG or JPEG file, the frequency (1 to 5) and Swap light and dark. The same inputs and the same seed give the same picture. Four new templates (Name collage, Two words, dense, Word and logo, Four brands mixed): the Pattern Studio now has 40 kinds and 80 templates.

Montserrat typeface

Besides DejaVu Sans and DejaVu Serif, the slim Montserrat is now supplied (Regular and Bold, SIL Open Font License 1.1), reduced to the characters of Western Europe. It is the default for newly created words of the collage; in the program, any system font is available as well. If a typeface lacks a character (Cyrillic or Greek in Montserrat), the collage says so with a solution. Older files stay as they were: a word without a typeface setting is still set in DejaVu Sans.

Collage in the online generator

The collage is also available at ortino-cam.com/tools/generator, with words and logos, the three supplied typefaces, an area up to 1200 × 600 mm and parts from 4 mm in height. The generator thus offers 38 of the 40 kinds and 78 of the 80 templates; Text from holes and Image as hole grid are missing. For the preview, texts and logo images go to the server for calculation; it does not store them and does not write them to the operating log. Guide in the wiki

Portal on the phone

The bottom bar always has five entries: Home, Explore, Plus (upload) in the middle, Shop or Machines, and Profile or Sign in. Activity is no longer an entry of its own but a number on the profile picture. The Tools icon in the top bar combines kitchen, furniture and patterns; kitchen and furniture open the Web Designer straight in the right area. The portal’s privacy policy now mentions the generator and the custom templates in the browser.

Rights and millability

At the logo slot the program and the generator say: “Only use logos and images you have the rights to.” Anyone who enters their own names and logos is responsible for the rights. Letters with inner areas produce islands that fall out when cut through; the traffic light therefore does not show green for words, and Bridges for islands sets tabs. The four collage templates are the only ones that are not green. The slim typeface has thin strokes; for a 3.175 mm bit many openings are too narrow, and the collage is meant more for V-bits and engraving.

“Optimised” cutting

The button “Optimise cutting” stays in the Furniture and Kitchen Designer (before, it opened the sheet cutting dialog instead of the designer), sets the new method “Optimised” and reports before and after. It tries MaxRects and Guillotine with 48 perturbed orders each and takes the layout with the fewest sheets and, in a tie, the one with the emptier last sheet. Measured on 30 random cases on the computer: never worse than the fixed methods, once one sheet fewer, 15 times a larger offcut. The gain is usually small. The saw plan has its own button Saw plan …. Files with “Optimised” cannot be opened in older program versions.

Creator: honestly speaking

  • Nothing milled, nothing glued, nothing assembled or hung up. All creators are tested only with Rust tests, Vitest tests, the simulator and Chromium.
  • The clearance (guide value 0.1 mm), web widths, gear flanks, belt grooves, the ruler’s graduation and the legibility of the QR code are calculated values and not tested on real material. First a test cut in an offcut and the fit test coupon.

  • Not included: animals as slot figures, the corner dovetail as on drawers, helical gearing. Only in the program and for Claude, not in the portal’s Web Designer. Guide in the wiki

Order of the depth passes: part by part or level by level

With several parts and more than one depth pass, for profile and pocket you choose under “Advanced” whether each part is milled to its final depth before the next one follows (part by part), or whether all parts first get the first depth pass, then the second (level by level). The default stays the previous behaviour: part by part for the profile, level by level for the pocket.

  • Why: until the last step all parts stay attached to the material; with tabs or a remaining layer the sheet stays stable.
  • What it costs: more idle moves, and if you abort, no part is finished. Measured on three parts, four depth passes, bit Ø 6 mm: rapid travel 1053 instead of 2006 mm, estimated time 70 instead of 89 seconds (calculation in the program, not milled).
  • Existing projects do not change: a test on eleven supplied examples and on the example project shows the same G-code byte for byte.
  • Tested only against Rust tests, the program’s own simulation and Chromium, not on a machine.

Correction: arcs over 180°

Fixed: the bounding box (size and position on the sheet) and the resolution into lines calculated arcs with an opening angle of more than 180° wrongly. Such arcs are now first split into two equal halves on the same circle; shape and area do not change as a result. It became visible with the creator parts (circular segments), but it affects every outline with such an arc. Whether projects from 0.12.0 were affected has not been investigated.

Features

One application for design, CAM, simulation and machine operation, with help for beginners and tools for advanced users.

✏️

Design

Shapes, text (also on a path, single-line fonts, as a stencil with bridges), Bézier pen, nodes, Boolean operations, offset, arrays (also along a path), serial numbers in texts, variables and formulas with units, parametric sketches with constraints and a history (not compared with a CAD program), extended snapping (intersections, extension, perpendicular, tangent, polar) and dimensioning. Import of SVG, DXF, PDF/AI, EPS/PostScript, images and 3D models (STL, STEP: as a relief, outline or layered model); Béziers from SVG and PDF and splines from DXF stay curves; export also as STL and 3MF (tested only with self-made files, likewise STL and STEP; 3MF not with a slicer).

🧭

Toolpaths

Profile, pocket (with a selectable order of the depth passes: part by part or level by level, since 0.13.0), adaptive clearing, drilling, thread milling (simulator only, not tested on a machine), V-carving, inlay, engraving and 3D relief. Post-processors for GRBL (tested on the Ultimate Bee), grblHAL, FluidNC, Mach3/4 and LinuxCNC, plus five more from manufacturer documentation. Apart from GRBL, none has been tested on a real machine.

🧊

3D simulation

Play back the material removal, detect collisions and oversize, estimate the time.

🧩

Patterns & relief

40 parametric perforation pattern kinds with 80 templates and a millability traffic light (since 0.14.0 with a search field and the new category Ornament, since 0.16.0 with the word and logo collage); 3D relief from generators (62 templates in 9 categories, 22 of them new in 0.14.0), shapes, greyscale images or a photo (Photo → relief with a local AI depth model), modelling with the brush, key figures for depth, steepness and inner radius, roughing and finishing with one click. Since 0.15.0 with the “Own” tab (save custom templates, back up as a file, import) and the “Shapes” dialog. Not tested on a real machine.

Machine connection

GRBL-compatible over USB/COM or TCP: manual control, homing, probing with a touch plate or XYZ corner block, tool change with a length sensor, touch plate or fixed probe. Pre-flight check

Travel, homing, work zero, tool, dust extraction and more are checked before every run. The program never writes settings into the controller.

📷

Camera on the worktable

Four QR marks in the corners, the camera image is rectified and lies millimetre-exact under the design; detect workpiece outline. Tested only with generated images, never with a real camera.

🛠️

Maintenance & tool life

Tool life limits per tool and a maintenance plan per machine, with notes in the pre-flight check. Guide values, simulator only.

📱

Controlled by Claude: MCP server and local API

ortino.CAM comes with an MCP server (Model Context Protocol). Claude Desktop, Claude Code and any other MCP client can draw, create operations, work through the checklist and generate G-code, live in the program window, every action one undo step.

  • MCP server ortino-mcp for Claude Desktop and Claude Code.
  • Local API (JSON, 127.0.0.1 only, random access key, switched on only on request) for your own programs and scripts.
  • Command line ortino: generate G-code, check a project, export DXF/SVG, series from CSV, also for CI.
  • Own tools as Rhai scripts that Claude can use too.
  • Assistant in the program with your own Anthropic API key (stored in the Windows Credential Manager).

need a fourth axis (rotary), want to model extensively in 3D (sculpting, components), work on a Mac (through Windows virtualisation), want manufacturer support or rely on long practice and a large user community.

want to run a GRBL machine straight from the program, are looking for a Furniture and Kitchen Designer with a parts list, want to use the community portal, want to drive your CAM with Claude (MCP) and prefer a free program, and can live with young software.

Installation

  1. Under Releases download the file ortino.cam_<Version>_x64-setup.exe (alternatively the .msi, …_x64_de-de.msi). The checksum (SHA-256) is in the notes of the version.
  2. Start it and install. Windows warns on the first start (SmartScreen: “More info → Run anyway”): the installer is signed with a self-made certificate, not one from a certificate authority.
  3. .ocam projects then open with a double click.

Updates come in the program itself through the operator’s portal (not through GitHub): the top bar shows a notice; it is installed only after your click, with a verified signature and never in the middle of a milling job. Only the system type, architecture, installation type and the installed version are transmitted.

Android app: there is an app for ortino.CAM in the Google Play Store: ortino.CAM at Google Play. What it can do is stated in the listing there; these pages deliberately make no statement about it. The Windows program does not need it.

Frequently asked questions

Does ortino.CAM cost anything?

No. Private and commercial use are allowed free of charge (terms in the file LICENSE in the repository). The source code is not public.

Does it run on Mac or Linux?

No, currently only on Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit).

Do I need Claude or an Anthropic key?

No. Everything essential runs without. You can additionally use the MCP server and the assistant in the program; the assistant needs your own Anthropic key.

Does the online generator produce G-code? Do I need an account for it?

No and no. The shape and pattern generator in the portal (since 0.15.0) needs no account and delivers SVG and DXF in millimetres, no G-code. G-code is only created in ortino.CAM: import the file, choose bit and operation. Whether a pattern holds in your material is not something the page says; its traffic light only measures the geometry on the computer.

Does it work with my machine?

With GRBL-compatible controllers (GRBL 1.1, grblHAL, FluidNC), yes, straight from the program. So far only the Ultimate Bee with xPRO V5 has been tested; everything else is not tested on a real machine. For Mach3/Mach4, LinuxCNC, UCCNC, Centroid, ShopBot, MASSO and PlanetCNC the program produces G-code for export; none of these post-processors has been tested on a real machine, and the last five are written from manufacturer documentation only. Check the dimensions in the machine profile and run first without tool and material.

Why does Windows warn when installing?

The installer is signed with a self-made certificate, not one from a certificate authority. Windows (SmartScreen) therefore shows a warning: “More info → Run anyway”. The checksum (SHA-256) is in the notes of every version.

Are the suggested feed rates and spindle speeds safe?

No, they are guide values. They depend on your machine, your bit and your material. Check them, start carefully and try on an offcut.

Can I take my bit library with me?

The program comes with over 250 bits and can read in tool databases from other programs (Vectric .vtdb, Fusion, CSV). The .vtdb format is not officially documented and has not been tested with a real Vectric file so far.

What data does the program send?

For the update check only the system type, architecture, installation type and the installed version, to the operator’s portal (not to GitHub). You talk to the community portal only when you use it. The assistant talks to Anthropic if you switch it on with your key. The local API is off until you switch it on, and reachable only on 127.0.0.1.

How can I help?

Most valuable are tests on real machines and bug reports, best as an issue on GitHub.
